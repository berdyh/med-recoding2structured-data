

## **Sample GP Consultation Transcript**

**Scenario:** Suspected **acute sinus infection** (sinusitis). Patient has been self-medicating.

---

**GP:** Hello, I’m Dr. Keller. What brings you in today?

**Patient:** Hi. I’ve had a blocked nose and facial pressure for about a week, and the pain around my cheeks has gotten worse in the last two days.

**GP:** Sorry to hear that. Is the pain on one side or both?

**Patient:** Mostly on the left side, especially when I lean forward.

**GP:** Any fever?

**Patient:** Yes, last night it was around 38.2°C.

**GP:** Do you have a cough or sore throat?

**Patient:** A little cough, but nothing major. The main thing is the pressure and the headache.

**GP:** Any nasal discharge? If so, what does it look like?

**Patient:** Yes, it’s thick and yellow-green.

**GP:** Have you taken any medication for this so far?

**Patient:** I’ve been taking over-the-counter decongestants and paracetamol. Also used a nasal spray for three days, but it didn’t help much.

**GP:** When you say nasal spray, do you mean a decongestant spray like oxymetazoline?

**Patient:** Yes, exactly that one.

**GP:** Have you had any sinus infections before?

**Patient:** Once, about two years ago.

**GP:** Let me examine your nose and sinuses. Is that alright?

**Patient:** Sure.

*(Short examination: tenderness over left maxillary sinus, swollen nasal mucosa, congested nasal passages)*

**GP:** You do have tenderness over the left sinus and a lot of congestion.

**Patient:** So it’s probably a sinus infection?

---

## **Diagnosis & Plan (separated)**

**Assessment:**
Findings suggest **acute bacterial sinusitis**, likely following a viral upper-respiratory infection. Persistent symptoms for >7 days, purulent discharge, unilateral facial pain, and fever support a bacterial cause.

**Plan:**

* Start **amoxicillin–clavulanate** for 7 days (or doxycycline if penicillin-allergic).
* Continue **paracetamol** for pain/fever.
* Stop the **decongestant nasal spray**—risk of rebound congestion when used >3 days.
* Switch to **saline rinses** and a **steroid nasal spray** (fluticasone) for congestion.
* Rest, hydration, humidified air.
* Return if symptoms worsen, if vision changes occur, or if severe facial swelling develops.
