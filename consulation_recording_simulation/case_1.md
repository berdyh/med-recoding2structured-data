## **Sample GP Consultation Transcript**

**GP:** Good morning, I’m Dr. Patel. How can I help today?

**Patient:** Hi. My lower back has been really painful since yesterday morning. It got worse overnight.

**GP:** Can you describe the pain? Sharp, dull, or more like stiffness?

**Patient:** It’s a sharp pain when I move, especially when bending or getting up from a chair.

**GP:** Did anything trigger it? Heavy lifting, awkward movement, exercise?

**Patient:** I helped move some furniture two days ago. The pain started the next morning.

**GP:** Any pain going down your legs? Numbness, tingling, or weakness?

**Patient:** No, it stays in my lower back.

**GP:** Trouble controlling your bladder or bowels?

**Patient:** No, nothing like that.

**GP:** Are you able to walk normally?

**Patient:** Yes, but slowly.

**GP:** Have you taken anything for the pain?

**Patient:** Just ibuprofen this morning. It helped a bit.

**GP:** I’d like to examine your back—check your range of motion and look for tenderness. Is that okay?

**Patient:** Yes, go ahead.

*(Brief physical exam)*

**GP:** You have muscle spasm and tenderness on the left side of your lower back. Your leg strength and nerve reflexes are normal.

**Patient:** Okay. What does that mean?

---

## **Diagnosis & Plan (separated as requested)**

**Assessment:**
Findings are consistent with **acute mechanical lower-back strain**, likely from lifting. No red-flag symptoms suggesting nerve compression or serious underlying disease.

**Plan:**

* Continue **NSAIDs** (ibuprofen) as needed, with food.
* Add gentle **heat** and **light mobility** rather than full rest.
* Short course of **muscle relaxants** if pain limits movement.
* Avoid heavy lifting for 1–2 weeks.
* Return immediately if leg weakness, numbness, bowel/bladder changes, or worsening severe pain occur.
