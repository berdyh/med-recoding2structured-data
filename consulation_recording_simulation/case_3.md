

## **Sample GP Consultation Transcript**

**Scenario:** Suspected **gastritis** (stomach inflammation).

---

**GP:** Good afternoon, I’m Dr. Ibrahim. What brings you in today?

**Patient:** Hi. I’ve been having this burning pain in my upper stomach for about five days.

**GP:** Does it get better or worse with eating?

**Patient:** It gets worse a couple of hours after I eat. Sometimes I feel nauseous too.

**GP:** Any vomiting or changes in your bowel habits?

**Patient:** No vomiting. Stools are normal.

**GP:** Have you noticed any black, tar-like stools or blood?

**Patient:** No, nothing like that.

**GP:** Do you drink alcohol, smoke, or take any medication regularly?

**Patient:** I don’t smoke. I drink occasionally. But I’ve been taking ibuprofen for a shoulder strain for about two weeks.

**GP:** How often?

**Patient:** Usually twice a day. Sometimes three times if it really hurts.

**GP:** That’s useful to know. Have you tried anything for the stomach pain?

**Patient:** I tried some antacids from the pharmacy. They help for a short time but the pain comes back.

**GP:** Any fever, chest pain, or trouble swallowing?

**Patient:** No.

**GP:** Let me examine your abdomen—just lie back for a moment.

*(Exam: mild tenderness in the epigastric area, no guarding, normal bowel sounds)*

**GP:** Your abdomen is soft, and the tenderness is mainly in the upper middle part.

**Patient:** So what do you think this is?

---

## **Diagnosis & Plan (separated)**

**Assessment:**
Features are consistent with **NSAID-induced gastritis** (stomach lining irritation from regular ibuprofen use). No signs suggesting bleeding or ulcer complications at this stage.

**Plan:**

* Stop **ibuprofen** immediately; switch to **paracetamol** for shoulder pain.
* Start a **proton pump inhibitor** (e.g., omeprazole) once daily for 2–4 weeks.
* Avoid alcohol, very spicy foods, coffee, and late meals.
* Warn about red-flag symptoms: black stools, vomiting blood, severe persistent pain—seek urgent review if these appear.
* Review in 1–2 weeks if symptoms are not improving.
